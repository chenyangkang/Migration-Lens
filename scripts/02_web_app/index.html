About this journey

How the view is reconstructed

The camera follows measured GPS positions. Travel direction comes from the track; it does not measure where the bird turned its head. Between observations within the journey’s interpolation limit, position and available altitude are interpolated. Crane altitude is unavailable, so its first-person camera uses an illustrative height, clearly labelled. Goose and crane speeds are segment averages estimated from the track. With “Show jump notices” checked, playback holds briefly before each long gap and explains the missing interval, then jumps to the next recorded fix. Use “Pause here” to inspect it. Uncheck “Show jump notices” for immediate, quiet jumps; you can still inspect gaps through “Recording gaps”. Hatched timeline areas and dashed map links mark gaps; the dashed line does not reconstruct a flight path.

Satellite imagery and terrain describe the landscape, but were not necessarily captured on the flight date. Recorded GPS altitude can contain errors. This is a geographical reconstruction, not video from the bird.

Local time and sunlight

The Local clock follows the bird’s nearest recorded location using IANA time zones and daylight-saving rules for the recorded date. UTC is always available. Over the ocean, nautical zones follow longitude. Time-zone boundaries are modern; historical political borders are not reconstructed.

Day/night lighting follows the sun’s position at the playback timestamp. Sunrise, twilight and night change the sky and terrain. “Daylight view” keeps the landscape visible for exploration. The light model does not reproduce clouds, weather, artificial lights or the bird’s night vision.

“24h light preview” holds the current track position and moves the lighting clock through 24 hours. It is a sunlight demonstration, not an extra day of bird movement. Normal playback skips long GPS gaps; seeking into one holds the last recorded position.

Historical weather overlays

Choose wind, precipitation, temperature, any combination, or none. These are hourly ERA5 reanalysis estimates supplied by Open-Meteo from Copernicus/ECMWF, licensed CC BY 4.0. Wind is at 10 m and temperature at 2 m above the surface; conditions at flight altitude can differ. ERA5 has a native 0.25° grid; the viewer uses a coarser 1° corridor sampling grid. Colours and arrows are analytical overlays, not simulated clouds or rain in the bird’s vision.

Point readings use the most recent hourly model timestamp and spatial interpolation between bordering samples. Coloured cells and regional arrows show the values at the 1° sample nodes; nearby wind arrows interpolate the same field. Blue precipitation shading is the preceding-hour total, including snow as water equivalent. Missing values stay unavailable. White arrows point in the direction the wind travels; larger arrows indicate stronger wind. The “from” direction in the reading follows meteorological convention. The weather map follows the bird when layers are enabled. Light preview keeps the weather at the original flight time. During a GPS gap, weather refers to the held last-fix location, not the bird’s unknown position.

Explore the surroundings

“Street View nearby” opens a ground-level panorama near the current bird location in Google Maps, where coverage exists. It shows the surroundings from the panorama’s capture position, rather than the bird’s flight height.

Keyboard: Space plays or pauses; arrow keys turn the view; R faces forward. Scroll over the flight view to change the field of view.
